I am a PhD student in Computer Science at Youngsan University, advised by Prof. Minpo Jung. I work on computer vision under low light — the regime where photons are scarce, sensor noise dominates, and the assumptions that ordinary detectors are trained on quietly stop holding.

My current work asks a question that most low-light pipelines skip: enhancement is normally optimised to look good to a human, then bolted onto a detector that was never consulted. What should enhancement do when its only consumer is a detector? That question is the subject of DOLENet, my first-author manuscript in preparation.

Before starting graduate study I spent four years as a Senior Software Instructor, designing and teaching full-semester computer science courses at roughly eleven universities and enterprise training programmes, to more than a thousand learners. I still consider teaching part of the job rather than a detour from it — see the teaching page.

I completed my MSc at Youngsan University (GPA 4.4 / 4.5) with a thesis on deep-learning low-light image enhancement, and my BEng at Concord University College, Fujian Normal University.
